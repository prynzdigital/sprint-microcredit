Before You Borrow: How to Determine Whether a Loan Is Affordable

By Sprint Micro-Credit4 min readPublished 15 Nov 2026

Unexpected expenses, family responsibilities and personal financial goals can sometimes make borrowing seem like the easiest solution. However, before taking a loan, one important question deserves careful consideration: can you comfortably afford to repay it?

Being eligible for a loan does not automatically mean that the loan amount is suitable for your financial situation.

1. Understand your monthly income

Start by considering how much you actually take home after statutory deductions and other regular deductions from your salary. Your take-home pay gives you a clearer picture of the money available to manage your monthly responsibilities.

2. Consider your existing expenses

Make a list of your regular expenses, including:

  • Rent and utility bills.
  • Food and transportation.
  • School fees and family responsibilities.
  • Existing loan repayments, especially loans that are not deducted on your payslip.
  • Other recurring financial commitments.

Understanding these expenses helps you determine how much of your income is already committed. Our budget planner can help.

3. Evaluate the proposed repayment

Before accepting a loan, understand the repayment amount, duration, interest and any applicable fees. Consider how the repayment will affect your remaining monthly income.

A repayment that appears manageable today should also be considered against your expected expenses throughout the loan period.

4. Borrow only what you need

It can be tempting to request the highest amount available, especially when additional funds seem useful. However, borrowing more than necessary can increase your repayment obligations.

Consider your actual financial need and avoid taking on commitments that may put unnecessary pressure on your income.

5. Leave room for unexpected expenses

Life is unpredictable. Medical expenses, family emergencies and other unexpected situations can affect your financial plans. Where possible, maintain some financial flexibility rather than committing every available cedi to loan repayments. An emergency fund helps.

Final thoughts

A good borrowing decision is not simply about how much money you can access. It is also about how comfortably you can repay it without disrupting your financial stability.
