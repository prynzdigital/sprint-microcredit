How to budget your salary so it lasts the whole month

By Sprint Micro-Credit6 min readUpdated 29 Oct 2026

Payday feels great until the third week, when the money is gone and the month isn’t. If that sounds familiar, you’re not alone. The fix isn’t earning more first. It’s giving every cedi a job before you spend it.

Why salaries “disappear”

Most money leaks come from small, frequent spending that never gets written down: daily data bundles, lunch, a quick top-up for a relative, ride-hailing when trotro would do. Separately they feel harmless. Together they can take 15–25% of a salary.

The other culprit is irregular big costs that we treat as surprises even though they come every year: school fees in September and January, Christmas, rent advances, funerals and car servicing.

The 50/30/20 rule, Ghana-style

A simple starting point is to split your take-home pay (after tax, SSNIT and deductions) into three buckets:

  • 50% Needs: rent, food, transport (trotro, fuel), electricity (ECG prepaid), water, school fees, essential family support, loan repayments.
  • 30% Wants: eating out, outings, new clothes, entertainment, extra data and subscriptions.
  • 20% Savings & goals: emergency fund, investments (T-bills, mutual funds), a susu group, or extra payments on debt.

Reality check: in Accra, rent and transport alone can push “needs” above 50%. That’s okay. Treat 50/30/20 as a target, not a test. Start by saving at least 10% and grow from there.

5 habits that make budgets stick

  1. Pay yourself first. On payday, move your savings to a separate account or MoMo wallet before you spend anything.
  2. Create “sinking funds”. Divide yearly costs by 12. If school fees are GH₵3,600 a year, set aside GH₵300 every month.
  3. Buy data bundles monthly, not daily. Daily bundles usually cost far more per GB.
  4. Set a family-support limit. Supporting family is part of our culture. Budget for it so it doesn’t crowd out your own needs.
  5. Review weekly. Spend 10 minutes each Sunday checking your MoMo and bank transactions against your plan.

When emergencies still happen: a well-planned short-term loan can bridge the gap. Just make sure the repayment fits inside your budget. Try our loan calculator with affordability check.
