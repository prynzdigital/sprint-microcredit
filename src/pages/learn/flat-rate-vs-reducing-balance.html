Flat rate vs reducing balance: what you really pay

By Sprint Micro-Credit5 min readUpdated 29 Oct 2026

Two loans can both say “5% per month” and still cost you very different amounts. The difference is how the interest is calculated. Once you understand this, you can compare any loan offer in Ghana with confidence.

Flat rate: interest on the original amount

With a flat rate, interest is charged on the full amount you borrowed, every month, even as you repay it.

Example: borrow GH₵2,000 at 5% flat per month for 4 months.
Interest = 2,000 × 5% × 4 = GH₵400.
Total repayable = GH₵2,400, or GH₵600 per month.

Flat rates are common for short-term loans because they’re simple. You know your fixed payment and total cost from day one.

Reducing balance: interest on what you still owe

With a reducing balance rate, interest is charged only on the amount still outstanding. As you repay, the interest portion shrinks.

For the same GH₵2,000 at 5% per month over 4 months, total interest on a reducing balance is roughly GH₵256, noticeably less than GH₵400.

So which is better?

At the same quoted number, reducing balance is cheaper. But lenders rarely quote the same number for both. A 5% flat rate might be cheaper than a 9% reducing rate. That’s why the golden rule is:

Always compare the total amount you will repay, including all fees, not just the rate or the monthly payment.

Questions to ask any lender

  • Is the rate flat or reducing balance? Per month or per year?
  • What fees apply (processing, insurance, disbursement)?
  • What is the total amount I will repay?
  • What happens if I pay late, or if I want to pay early?
