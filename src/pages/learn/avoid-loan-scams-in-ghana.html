7 signs of a loan scam in Ghana

By Sprint Micro-Credit5 min readUpdated 29 Oct 2026

When you urgently need money, scammers know you’re more likely to act fast. Learn these warning signs and share them with family and colleagues. It could save someone a lot of pain.

1. They ask you to pay before you get the loan

“Processing fee”, “insurance”, “activation”, “release fee”: if someone asks you to send money by MoMo before your loan is paid, stop. Legitimate lenders disclose fees in a written agreement, and any fees are typically deducted from the loan or added to repayments, not sent to a personal wallet.

2. The offer is “guaranteed” with no checks

Real lenders check who you are and whether you can repay. “No documents, guaranteed approval, any amount” is bait.

3. You’re contacted out of the blue

Unsolicited WhatsApp messages, SMS or Facebook DMs offering loans are a major red flag, especially from personal numbers.

4. The account copies a real company

Scammers copy logos and names of real lenders, including ours. Always check the phone number and page against the official website. Sprint Micro-Credit’s only official number is {{site.phoneDisplayIntl}}, and our email is {{site.email}}.

5. There’s no physical address

A genuine lender has an office you can visit. Ours is at {{site.address.street}}, {{site.address.locality}}, Accra.

6. They pressure you to decide now

“Offer expires in 30 minutes!” Urgency stops you from thinking clearly. A real lender will give you time to read your agreement.

7. The app wants access to everything

Some predatory loan apps request access to your contacts and photos, then harass your friends and family if you’re late. Check app permissions carefully and only use lenders you can verify.

Rule of thumb: never send money to get money. Never share your MoMo PIN or one-time codes (OTP) with anyone, including people who claim to be from your network, your bank or a lender.

How to verify a lender

  • Check that the lender is licensed by the Bank of Ghana through the BoG website, and ask for their licence details.
  • Call the number on their official website, not the one in the message you received.
  • Visit their office if you can.
  • Read the written agreement before paying or signing anything.

If you’ve been targeted

  1. Stop all contact and don’t send any more money.
  2. Call your mobile network’s customer service immediately to report the fraudulent transaction and number.
  3. Report to the Ghana Police Service and your bank if bank details were involved.
  4. Warn friends and family. Scammers often move through contact lists.
