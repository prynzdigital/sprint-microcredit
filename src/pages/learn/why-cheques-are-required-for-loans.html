Why Are Cheques Required When Applying for a Loan?

By Sprint Micro-Credit3 min readPublished 15 Nov 2026

When applying for a salary loan, borrowers may be asked to provide certain documents to support their application. One requirement that sometimes raises questions is the provision of cheques covering the loan repayment period.

For some applicants, this may seem unusual, especially when the loan does not require physical collateral. So, why are cheques required?

Understanding the purpose of cheques

Cheques may form part of the repayment-security arrangements agreed between a borrower and a lender. Where a borrower fails to meet agreed repayment obligations, a lender may be able to present a relevant cheque, subject to the terms of the loan arrangement and applicable law.

For a lender that does not require traditional physical collateral, this can be one element of its approach to managing repayment risk. However, providing a cheque does not remove the borrower’s responsibility to make regular repayments as agreed, and it should not be treated as a substitute for understanding the loan terms.

Why is it important to complete cheques properly?

A cheque is a financial instrument, and the information written on it matters. Borrowers should ensure that all required details are completed accurately and consistently with the agreed loan documentation before signing and submitting it.

Leaving important sections blank can create uncertainty and potential disagreements later, particularly if there is a dispute over information entered after signing.

To protect yourself:

  • Ensure the required cheque details are completed accurately before signing.
  • Verify the amount, date, payee and other relevant details.
  • Do not sign or hand over a blank or incomplete cheque.
  • Understand the purpose of each cheque and the circumstances in which it may be presented.
  • Ask for clarification if any part of the cheque arrangement is unclear.
  • Keep a record of the cheque details and the terms agreed with your lender.

Final thoughts

Cheques are not a replacement for responsible borrowing or regular loan repayments. They are an additional consideration within certain lending arrangements.

Before signing any financial document, ensure you understand what you are agreeing to and the responsibilities that come with it.
