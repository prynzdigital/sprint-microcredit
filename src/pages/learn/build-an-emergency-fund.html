Build an emergency fund on a Ghanaian salary

By Sprint Micro-Credit5 min readUpdated 29 Oct 2026

Yes, a lender is telling you to save. The best customers are people who rarely need emergency loans, because when they borrow it’s a choice, not a crisis. An emergency fund is how you get there.

How much do you need?

Add up your essential monthly costs: rent, food, transport, utilities, school fees and minimum loan repayments. Then aim for:

  • Starter goal: 1 month of essentials
  • Solid goal: 3 months
  • Strong goal: 6 months, especially if you support others or work on contract

Where to keep it

Your emergency fund must be safe and easy to reach within a day or two, but not so easy that you dip into it for shopping.

  • A separate savings account at a bank or savings & loans company, not the one your salary lands in.
  • Treasury bills (91-day / 182-day) for part of the fund, through your bank or a licensed broker.
  • Money market mutual funds from licensed fund managers, which usually let you withdraw within a few working days.

Keep at least one month’s worth somewhere you can reach the same day. Check that any investment company is licensed by the Securities and Exchange Commission (SEC) Ghana or the Bank of Ghana.

Ways to build it faster

  1. Automate it: set up a standing order on payday.
  2. Save windfalls: bonuses, allowances and gifts go straight into the fund.
  3. Join a trusted susu or savings group with people you know well.
  4. Round up: every time you spend GH₵45, save the GH₵5 to make it GH₵50.
