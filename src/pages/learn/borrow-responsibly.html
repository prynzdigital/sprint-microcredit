How to borrow responsibly (and when not to)

By Sprint Micro-Credit5 min readUpdated 29 Oct 2026

A loan is a tool. Used well, it gets you through an emergency or helps you seize an opportunity. Used badly, it eats next month’s salary before you’ve earned it. Here’s how to stay on the right side.

Good reasons to borrow

  • Genuine emergencies: medical bills, urgent repairs, a family crisis.
  • Time-sensitive needs: school fees due before payday, a rent advance to secure a good apartment.
  • Things that pay you back: a course that raises your income, stock for a side business with proven sales.

Reasons to pause

  • Lifestyle spending: parties, the latest phone, impressing others.
  • Borrowing to repay another loan you already can’t afford. Talk to that lender first.
  • When you don’t know the total amount you’ll repay.

The 30% rule

Add up all your monthly loan repayments, including salary deductions, hire-purchase and MoMo loans. Keep the total under about 30% of your take-home pay. Above 40%, most people start missing other essentials.

Example: take-home pay GH₵5,000 → aim to keep all repayments under GH₵1,500/month. If you already pay GH₵800 on another loan, a new loan should cost no more than about GH₵700 a month.

Our loan calculator has a built-in affordability meter that does this check for you.

Choose the shortest term you can afford

Longer terms mean smaller monthly payments but more total interest. Find the monthly payment you can comfortably manage, then pick the shortest period that fits it.

Before you sign, ask

  1. What is the total amount I will repay?
  2. Is the rate flat or reducing, monthly or yearly? (Why it matters)
  3. What fees are included, and are any deducted from what I receive?
  4. What are the penalties for late payment? Can I repay early?
  5. Is the lender properly registered, and can I visit their office?

Protect your credit record

In Ghana, lenders share repayment information with licensed credit reference bureaus. Paying on time builds a record that helps you access bigger, cheaper credit later. Missing payments does the opposite.

If you can see trouble coming, talk to your lender early. It’s much easier to agree a solution before a payment is missed than after.
